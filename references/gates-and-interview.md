# Gates and Interview

> **Use when**: Gate G1's three-line goal is not penetrating, or the user gives only one line of direction and the matter needs to be asked through.
> **Triggers**: three-line goal, who it is for, what problem it solves, what counts as done, Socratic, interview, full 6 questions, short 3 questions, assumption.
> **Path**: `skills/superpowers/skills/project-kickoff-prep/references/gates-and-interview.md`
> **Boundary**: Only manages clarifying the goal; does not judge whether the project is worth doing, does not fill in the requirements template on the user's behalf.

## I. Goal in three lines (output of gate G1)

Present the three lines to the user for line-by-line confirmation; adopt whatever the user has already given directly, without rephrasing and re-asking.

    Goal in three lines:
    1. Who it is for: [specific person, not "everyone"]
    2. What problem it solves: [his current pain, one sentence]
    3. What counts as done: [a verifiable criterion, not "works well"]

- If it cannot be answered → mark 【Assumption】, write it into the project brief and add it to the items to confirm; never fabricate, never take irreversible action on an assumption.
- If the three lines are imprecise, or still vague after 3 questions → escalate to the full 6-question version in §III.

## II. Short 3-question version (AI asks the user)

One question at a time, at most 3 questions, decide the next question from the answer; if vague, keep drilling to specifics:

1. Who is this for? (specific person, not "everyone")
2. What problem does it solve for him? (his current pain, one sentence)
3. What counts as done? (a verifiable criterion, not "works well")

Stop as soon as there is enough information; no need to fill all 3 questions. The output is exactly the three-line goal in §I.

## III. Full 6-question version (the user can also initiate this with any AI)

My confusion is: [describe as specifically as possible what happened, how you understand it, and where you are stuck].
Do not give advice yet. Please conduct a Socratic interview with me, and through at most 6 questions help me find the question truly worth answering.

Please follow these rules: 1. Ask only one question at a time, decide the next from my answer, do not hand me a whole questionnaire in advance; 2. prioritize distinguishing whether what I say is a verifiable fact, an interpretation of the fact, a value judgment, or a goal I want to achieve; 3. check whether keywords are vague, what premises I have assumed, where the evidence comes from, whether there is an opposite explanation, and what it means if the conclusion holds or fails; 4. before each question, use one sentence to say what judgment my previous answer updated; 5. only ask questions that could change the conclusion. Stop as soon as there is enough information; no need to fill all 6.

After the interview, please organize: 1. the question I originally asked; 2. the problem I truly want to solve; 3. facts already confirmed; 4. assumptions still unverified; 5. the key variable most likely to change the conclusion; 6. one accurate, specific, actionable new question.

Wait until I confirm this new question before giving your judgment, reasoning, and next steps.
